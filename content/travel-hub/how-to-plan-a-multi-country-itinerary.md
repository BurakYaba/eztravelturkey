**The short answer:** keep the route geographic, limit the number of hotel changes, and give each major stop at least two nights. Then let one team coordinate flights, ferries and transfers.

## 1. Choose a logical order

Move in one direction instead of doubling back. Turkey to Greece, or Greece to Egypt, follows natural flight paths and avoids wasted days.

## 2. Don't over-pack the schedule

A multi-country trip tempts you to add more. Fewer places with more time is usually better. As a rule of thumb, allow two to three nights for each main region.

## 3. Use domestic and regional flights

Short flights replace long road days. They also make it easier to pair countries that would otherwise take a full day to cross.

## 4. Match the season

Choose a window that suits all your destinations. April to May and late September to October work for Turkey, Greece, Egypt and Jordan.

## 5. Keep a buffer

Weather affects ballooning in Cappadocia and ferries in Greece. A spare morning or an extra night before an international flight avoids stress.

## 6. Pick the right format

Private tours give flexibility. Small group tours are social and cost-effective. You can mix both within one trip.

## 7. Have one point of contact

Coordinating hotels, guides, ferries and flights across several countries is a lot of work. We handle it as a single itinerary, with 24/7 local support.

Popular combinations are Turkey and Greece, Turkey and Egypt, Turkey and Jordan, Greece and Egypt, and a four-country route.
